There's this idea that you should wait to reach out until you're feeling okay — cheerful enough, interesting enough, not a burden. So on the days you feel flat, or tired, or a little off, you close the app instead of opening a conversation. But some of the most human moments happen exactly on those days, if you let them.

## You're not required to perform

A conversation doesn't need you to bring energy you don't have. You can say "not doing great today" and just let that be the opening line, instead of a confession you have to apologize for. Most people won't mind — plenty of them are having a similar kind of day, quietly, without saying so first.

## Being present matters more than being fun

We often assume people want us to be entertaining, upbeat, easy to talk to. In practice, a lot of people just want someone else there — not performing, just present. A short, low-energy conversation where nobody's trying too hard can feel more honest than a lively one built on effort.

## Someone choosing to stay says something

When you're not at your best and someone still wants to keep talking, that says more than any compliment could. It's not about what gets said — it's the simple fact that they didn't leave when you gave them an easy excuse to.

## A place where you don't have to warm up first

On HEYID, there's no expectation to arrive in a great mood — you're talking to a stranger who has no picture of your "usual self" to compare tonight to. Automatic translation means even a tired, half-formed sentence gets across just fine. Some evenings, that's exactly the kind of conversation that helps the most.
