Travel is expensive, slow, and needs planning. A conversation with someone on the other side of the world needs none of that. It's the closest thing to teleportation that actually exists — not to a place, but into someone else's ordinary evening, in a country you've never been to.

## You don't need the plane ticket to get the shift in perspective

Most of what people actually get out of travel isn't the landmarks — it's the small, unplanned moments: overhearing how people talk to each other, noticing what's considered normal that isn't normal back home, realizing your own daily life is just one version of how things could be. A lot of that comes from talking to a local, not from seeing a monument. You can get that same shift from a conversation, minus the flight.

## The mundane details are the interesting part

Ask someone in another country what time they eat dinner, what their commute looks like, what's playing on the radio right now, and you'll learn more about how life actually works there than any guidebook could tell you. These aren't dramatic questions. They're just ordinary ones asked about an unfamiliar place, and that's exactly what makes them interesting.

## Somewhere to go without going anywhere

On HEYID, this is essentially the whole premise — a conversation partner is picked from anywhere, and automatic translation means the language on their end never gets in the way. You don't choose a destination. Someone else's ordinary evening, in whatever country they happen to be having it, becomes the destination for you.
