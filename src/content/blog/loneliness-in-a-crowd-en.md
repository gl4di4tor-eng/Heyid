You can live in a city where thousands of people walk past you every day and still feel like no one actually knows you exist. It's a strange feeling — it's not about the lack of people around you, it's about the lack of one conversation that actually means something.

Psychologists call this social loneliness — a state where the number of contacts you have has nothing to do with how understood you feel. You can have a full inbox and still feel empty. You can sit in a crowded room and feel more alone than you would in an empty one.

## Why it's sometimes easier with a stranger than a friend

It sounds counterintuitive, but plenty of people feel more comfortable talking to a total stranger than to someone they've known for years. There's no shared history to carry, no expectations, no old misunderstandings to work around. A stranger has no picture of you yet — they get to meet you exactly as you are right now, not as you were five years ago.

That's why conversations with people from the other side of the world can end up surprisingly honest. Nobody's judging anyone through the lens of a shared past, because there simply isn't one.

## Curiosity as a cure for loneliness

The biggest paradox of loneliness is that the most effective cure for it isn't looking for someone close — it's being curious about someone completely new. One good conversation with a stranger can remind you that you're still capable of connecting with people, and that nothing was actually wrong with you — you just needed a different context.

Apps like HEYID exist because of exactly this idea: that sometimes what we're missing isn't more friends, it's one conversation with someone who, that same evening, is also looking for another human being on the other side of the screen. Real-time translation just means that person can live in a completely different country — and it stops mattering.

## You don't need years to feel a little less alone

Closeness doesn't always take years to build. Sometimes twenty minutes of an honest conversation with someone whose name you won't even remember is enough to feel like the day wasn't wasted after all. It doesn't have to lead anywhere else. Sometimes it's enough that, for a moment, you stop being invisible.
