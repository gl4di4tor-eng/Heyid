You can know every grammar rule by heart and still freeze up the moment you need to use them in a real conversation. That's because textbook language and spoken-in-the-moment language are two different skills — and only one of them gets trained by actually talking.

## Why context sticks better than rules

When someone explains a word to you mid-conversation, rather than in an exercise, your brain files it away with the emotion and situation attached, not as an isolated rule. That's why you remember a word you heard in a funny moment far better than the same word from a vocabulary list.

## Mistakes in conversation teach more than correct test answers

In a real exchange, a mistake gets a natural, gentle correction on the spot — someone says "I think you meant..." — and that sticks harder than a red mark in a workbook ever could.

## Live translation isn't a shortcut around learning — it's a way in

It's easy to assume a translation-powered app like HEYID teaches nothing, since "it's all translated anyway." In practice it's the opposite — seeing the original phrase next to its translation gets you quietly matching words across both languages, without even trying.

## Consistency beats length

You don't need hour-long conversations. A few short chats a week with different people teach more than one long conversation once a month, because the brain holds onto things better when they repeat in small doses, in different contexts, with different accents.
