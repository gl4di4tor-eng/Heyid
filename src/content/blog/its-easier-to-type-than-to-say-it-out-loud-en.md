Some things are easier to type than to say out loud. Not because they matter less — sometimes it's the opposite. Text simply gives you something a spoken conversation doesn't: a moment to look a sentence over before it actually reaches someone.

## Your voice gives away more than you'd like

When we speak out loud, our voice shakes, we pause, hesitation is audible. Writing doesn't show any of that. You can type something difficult in a calm, measured tone, even if that's not how you actually feel inside — and sometimes that control is exactly what lets you say the thing at all.

## You can still fix it before you send it

In a spoken conversation, once a word is out, it's out. In text, you get a moment to delete the sentence, rephrase it, decide it's not the right time yet. That option to pull back, even if rarely used, is often what gives people the courage to write harder things in the first place.

## A stranger doesn't see your face

A text conversation with someone new doesn't require showing facial expressions, which often give away more emotion than the words themselves. That makes it easier to type something personal to a stranger in chat than to admit the same thing to someone watching your face change as you say it.

## It's not about avoiding hard topics

Typing instead of speaking isn't a way to dodge important conversations — sometimes it's the only way to start them at all. And a conversation that's started, even a written one, is better than one that never happened.
