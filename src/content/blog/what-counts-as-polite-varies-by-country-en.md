Asking someone directly how much they earn is rude in one country and completely normal in another. Silence during a conversation means discomfort in one culture and respect for the other person's words in another. There's no single universal code of politeness, even though each of us carries one around in our head as if it were obvious to everyone.

## Why it's easy to offend someone without meaning to

Most misunderstandings don't come from bad intentions — they come from assuming our own rules are the default. Someone who gets straight to the point might come across as cold to one person, while someone who spends a while on small talk might seem evasive to another.

## Curiosity instead of judgment

Instead of assuming something is rude, it's usually better to just ask. Talking to someone from a different culture is a rare chance to say outright, "is it normal where you're from to ask that right away?" — and that kind of honesty usually lands better than a silent assumption.

## The differences that surprise people most

How people greet each other, how fast they get to the point, how many compliments feel normal, even how long you're expected to hesitate before saying no — all of it varies by place. Talking regularly with people from different countries, like on HEYID, stops these differences from being confusing and starts making them genuinely interesting.

## One rule that works everywhere

If you're not sure, ask directly and with curiosity, not judgment. That one rule holds regardless of where the other person is from, and it usually leads to a better conversation than trying to guess what's "acceptable."
