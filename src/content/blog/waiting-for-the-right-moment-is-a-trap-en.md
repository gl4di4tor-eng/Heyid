There's always a reason to wait. You're tired, it's late, you don't know what you'd even say, it feels random. The right moment — calm, clear-headed, with something interesting to talk about — sounds like it's coming eventually. It almost never does.

## The right moment is a moving target

The problem with waiting for the right moment is that it's defined by hindsight, not by anything you can plan for. You only know a moment was right after a conversation already went well. Before that, every moment looks equally uncertain, which means "waiting for the right one" is really just waiting indefinitely.

## Most good conversations start at the wrong time

Ask people about a conversation that mattered to them, and it rarely started at a convenient moment. It started at 1am when they should've been asleep, or during a boring commute, or right when they were about to close the app. The timing wasn't right — the conversation just happened anyway, and turned out fine.

## Readiness is overrated

You don't need a topic prepared, a clear head, or good timing to start talking to someone. Most conversations find their own direction within the first few messages, regardless of how they started. Waiting to feel "ready" just delays a conversation that would have worked out on its own.
