Some conversations just run late, without either person planning it. You start talking about nothing in particular, and somehow it's past midnight and you're both still typing. Eventually someone writes "I should probably sleep" and then, a moment later, "goodnight, it was nice talking to you." And that's it. That's the whole ending.

## Not every conversation needs a sequel

There's a quiet kind of completeness in a conversation that ends cleanly, with no promise to talk again, no exchanging of usernames for later. It happened, it was good, and now it's over. That's not sad — it's just a different shape than the conversations we're used to keeping going.

## The goodnight is doing more than it seems

"Goodnight, it was nice talking to you" is a small sentence, but it closes something properly. It says the time mattered, without needing to say much else. You go to sleep having been part of someone else's evening, and them a part of yours, even though neither of you knows what the other looks like.

## Strangers can give you a good ending too

We usually think endings matter most with people who know us — a proper goodbye, a real closing to a friendship or relationship. But a stranger saying goodnight at the end of a good conversation gives you something similar, on a much smaller scale: a clean, warm close to something that existed for an hour and then didn't need to exist anymore.

## Where these late goodnights happen often

On HEYID, conversations don't come with a schedule — someone on the other side might be starting their morning while you're ending your night, and automatic translation means "goodnight" lands the same regardless of the hour or the language it's said in. Sometimes that's the whole point of the conversation: not where it goes, but how gently it ends.
