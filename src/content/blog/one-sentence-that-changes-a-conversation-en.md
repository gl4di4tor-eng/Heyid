Most conversations start the same way — where are you from, how's your day, what do you do. Perfectly fine questions, and also exactly the kind that lead nowhere interesting. Then, occasionally, someone asks something slightly off-script, and the whole conversation changes direction.

## Small talk answers small talk

Ask a predictable question and you'll get a predictable answer, because that's what the question is built for. "How's your day" invites "fine, you?" almost automatically. It's not that people are boring — it's that the question never asked for anything more than a polite reply.

## One odd question does more than ten normal ones

Something like "what's the last thing that made you laugh out loud" or "what's a weirdly specific thing you're good at" breaks the script just enough that the other person has to actually think for a second instead of reaching for a default answer. That half-second of thinking is usually where the interesting part of the conversation begins.

## It doesn't need to be clever, just specific

The best conversation-shifting questions aren't witty — they're just concrete instead of generic. Specific questions ask for a real memory or a real opinion, and real answers are almost always more interesting than polite ones.
