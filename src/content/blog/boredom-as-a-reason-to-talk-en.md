Most people wait for the "right moment" to meet someone new — some occasion, an event, a trip. Meanwhile, the most ordinary, least dramatic conversations tend to start exactly when nothing in particular is happening. On a Wednesday evening, when you've got nothing to do and your phone is just sitting there.

## Boredom lowers the barrier to entry

When we're busy, every new conversation needs a justification — a purpose, a reason, some point to it. When we're bored, that requirement disappears. You can message someone just because nothing else comes to mind, and oddly enough, that leads to looser, less forced conversations than the "planned" ones.

## The best conversations don't have an agenda

Conversations that start without a specific goal often end up going in completely unexpected directions. Nobody's trying to prove anything, nobody has a clear idea of where the conversation "should" be heading. That makes it easier to just be yourself, because there's no pressure about the outcome.

## Why the internet makes this easier than real life does

In real life, boredom rarely leads to meeting someone new — you'd have to leave the house, find an opportunity, push past some physical hesitation. Online, like on HEYID, that same boredom impulse can turn into a conversation within seconds, with whoever else happens to be sitting there bored with their phone too. Automatic translation means it doesn't matter where that other person actually is.

## Boredom isn't a problem to solve — it's an opportunity

Instead of treating boredom as something to kill off as fast as possible with a show or endless scrolling, it's sometimes worth just messaging someone you don't know. The worst that can happen is the conversation goes nowhere and you're back to the same boring evening you started with. The best that can happen is hard to predict until you actually try.
