You send a message, the other person replies, and then nothing. A minute, two, five. With someone you've known for years, that kind of silence often triggers worry — you start wondering if you said something wrong. With a stranger, it's different, even if we rarely notice it.

## Silence without history doesn't mean the same thing

With someone you know, every pause carries context — past conversations, known moods, a whole history your brain automatically pulls up to explain the silence. With a stranger, that history doesn't exist. The silence is just silence, without the baggage we usually attach to it ourselves.

## Sometimes it's a sign the conversation is comfortable, not that it's ending

Oddly enough, it's often in conversations that are going well that people allow themselves a longer pause — without typing "sorry, was busy" or explaining themselves. You don't need to fill every second with words for a conversation to still mean something.

## A stranger isn't waiting for an explanation

In a conversation with someone new, there's usually no expectation that you'll immediately justify why you didn't reply right away. That removes a kind of pressure that, in closer relationships, can make every silence feel like it needs a comment.

## Not every pause needs fixing

Sometimes the best thing you can do with silence in a conversation is just leave it there. The other person will come back when they have something to say — or they won't, and that's fine too.
